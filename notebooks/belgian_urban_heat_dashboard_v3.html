Projected Risk Indicators
Methodology: Forecast uses linear regression fitted on observed mean anomalies (2023–2025) extrapolated forward. Climate scenario offsets are applied from IPCC AR6 regional projections for Western Europe: RCP 2.6 (+0.1°C/decade), RCP 4.5 (+0.22°C/decade), RCP 8.5 (+0.38°C/decade). Urban heat island amplification factor +15% is applied vs rural RMI baselines.

Limitations: With only 3 years of training data, long-range projections carry high uncertainty. Confidence intervals widen substantially beyond 2028. This model should be updated annually as new Sentinel-2 scenes become available. The projected values represent the city mean anomaly — individual hotspot locations may exceed these by +2–4°C.
⚠ Geographic scope: Sentinel-2 imagery was downloaded for Leuven, Belgium only via the Copernicus Data Space Browser. The spatial heat anomaly maps therefore cover the Leuven AOI (50.84–50.94°N, 4.63–4.78°E). RMI AWS data extends the temporal comparison to 5 Belgian cities, but satellite-derived UHI maps are Leuven-specific.

🌿 Feature Engineering — NDVI & NDBI
Why these indices?
NDVI captures vegetation density — the primary cooling driver via evapotranspiration. NDBI captures impervious surface intensity — the primary warming driver via heat absorption. Both derived from downloaded B04, B08, B11 bands only.
IndexFormulaHigh value meansUHI effect
NDVI(B08−B04)/(B08+B04)Dense vegetationCooling ↓
NDBI(B11−B08)/(B11+B08)Built-up surfaceWarming ↑
WorldCover validation: ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 (terrascope.be) was used to verify NDVI/NDBI patterns match known land cover — city centre (built-up, red class) → high NDBI · Dijle valley (tree cover, dark green) → high NDVI.

🤖 ML Strategy — Train / Test / Retrain
Target: temp_anomaly = temp_c − city_daily_mean
Why anomaly, not absolute? Absolute temperature varies by ~1°C across the city spatially but ~17°C day-to-day. A model trained on absolute temperature learns weather, not land-use. Anomaly removes day-to-day weather, leaving only the spatial UHI signal (range: ~12°C).

🌡 What Do the Anomaly Colours Mean?
🔴 Red (positive anomaly) — location is hotter than the city average that day.
Caused by: dense concrete, asphalt, rooftops (low albedo ~0.1), absence of vegetation.
Typical range: +1°C to +4°C above city mean.

🔵 Blue (negative anomaly) — location is cooler than the city average.
Caused by: parks, tree canopy, rivers, gardens (evapotranspiration cooling).
Typical range: −1°C to −3°C below city mean.

⚪ White (zero anomaly) — at the city mean. Mixed land use or transitional zone.

Why is the grid map range narrow (−1 to +0.3°C)?
When predicting on the grid, HUMIDITY, ALTITUDE, wind_ms and SOLARRADIATION are fixed at Q3 medians — only NDVI and NDBI vary spatially. If the model gives weight to the fixed meteorological features, spatial variation is suppressed. The training anomaly spans −4.4 to +7.78°C; the grid understates this. Fix: substitute kriged weather surfaces for medians.
Key findings from the data:
🏙 Liège — consistently warmest Belgian city (2025: 19.4°C)
📈 Brussels — largest year-over-year spike (+0.9°C from 2023→2025)
🌿 Ghent — coolest city (canal network and green coverage)
🏫 Leuven — hotspots near city centre; cool zones in Dijle valley & university parks
