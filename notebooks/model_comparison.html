🔧 What Changed — v1 → v2

Two methodology fixes eliminate leakage; one new feature; one new ensemble architecture; five-city coverage.

🐛 Bug Fixes (methodology)

🔴
StandardScaler data leakage — fixedv1: scaler.fit_transform(all_data) before split. The scaler saw the test set's mean/std, inflating evaluation metrics. v2: scaler.fit(X_train), then scaler.transform(X_test).
🔴
LOSOCV per-fold scaler — fixedv1 reused the global scaler inside each LOSOCV fold, leaking the held-out station's stats into training. v2 fits a fresh scaler per fold. LOSOCV RMSE: 0.604 → 0.592.

✨ New Feature & Architecture

🟢
B11 (SWIR) — 7th feature addedRaw SWIR captures absolute thermal inertia. NDBI normalises it away (dense urban ÷ value). B11 raw keeps the scale, letting the model distinguish high-density impervious from sparse impervious at the same NDBI ratio.
🟢
IMPERVIOUSNESS (EU HRL 2018) — 8th feature addedSealed-surface fraction at 10 m resolution from the EEA ImageServer REST API. Becomes the dominant SHAP driver (#1, mean |SHAP| = 0.200) — permanently sealed surfaces directly suppress evaporative cooling and increase thermal mass. NDVI's SHAP importance collapsed 80% (0.189 → 0.037) once this direct measure replaced the proxy signal.
🟢
Stacked ensemble: Ridge meta-learnerOOF predictions from RF + XGBoost → Ridge. Weights: RF 0.365, XGB 0.685. OOF generation prevents leakage into the meta-learner. +0.011 R² over tuned XGBoost alone.

🔬 Feature Selection — How We Chose What to Keep

Every candidate feature was assessed against three criteria: physical plausibility, data availability at sensor resolution, and correlation with UHI anomaly.

FeatureSourceDecisionPhysical Reason
NDVI
(B08−B04)/(B08+B04)		Sentinel-2KEPTVegetation reduces UHI via evapotranspiration and shading. Strong negative correlation with anomaly (parks cool, bare soil/concrete hot).
NDBI
(B11−B08)/(B11+B08)		Sentinel-2KEPTBuilt-up index. Positive correlation with UHI — concrete and asphalt absorb and re-emit solar radiation. Normalised ratio strips absolute SWIR magnitude.
B11 (SWIR raw)
Band 11, 20 m		Sentinel-2ADDED v2Absolute Short-Wave Infrared reflectance. Dense urban cores (high building density, contiguous impervious cover) have higher B11 than sparse suburban at the same NDBI value. Restores signal compressed by normalisation.
HUMIDITYRMI / Leuven.coolKEPTEvaporative cooling effect. High humidity suppresses surface temperature gain; dry conditions amplify UHI. Station-level variable.
ALTITUDEDEM (station metadata)KEPTLapse rate ≈ 0.65°C per 100 m. Leuven's elevation range (10–90 m) produces up to 0.5°C systematic offset without this feature.
wind_msRMI / Leuven.coolKEPTWind ventilates urban canyons and disperses heat plumes. Strong negative UHI correlation in calm conditions (summer anticyclone).
SOLARRADIATIONRMI / Leuven.coolKEPTIncoming shortwave drives surface heating. Correlates with anomaly magnitude on clear-sky summer days (all training scenes are low-cloud <20%).
IMPERVIOUSNESS
EU HRL 2018, 10 m		EEA ImageServerADDED v2Sealed-surface fraction (0–100%) from the European Environment Agency High-Resolution Layer 2018. The dominant SHAP driver (#1, mean |SHAP| = 0.200). Directly encodes evaporative suppression and thermal mass — structural land-cover signal that spectral indices (NDBI, B11) can only approximate. NDVI's importance dropped 80% once this direct measure was available.
B04, B08 rawSentinel-2DROPPEDRedundant once NDVI is computed. Including raw bands alongside the ratio adds collinear features with no new information. Kept only in CNN image patches where spatial context matters.
B12 (SWIR-2)Sentinel-2DROPPEDPearson ρ(B11, B12) > 0.95 on summer scenes. Highly collinear with B11. Adding both increases noise without adding signal; B11 preferred (used in NDBI formula).
NDWI
(B03−B08)/(B03+B08)		Sentinel-2DROPPEDWater index has near-zero values everywhere except the Dijle river, which covers <0.5% of the grid. Adds noise for 99.5% of pixels.
LST (thermal)Landsat 8/9NOT USEDLand Surface Temperature from Landsat thermal band (Band 10, 30 m) would be ideal, but introduces a second satellite with different overpass times and resolution mismatch. Temporal alignment with Sentinel-2 scenes is complex. Candidate for v3.
Building height / DSMNGI BelgiumNOT USEDPhysically motivated (street canyon effect, sky-view factor), but requires preprocessing Belgium's Digital Surface Model from NGI at sensor resolution. Candidate for v3.
Population densityStatbel / EurostatNOT USEDAvailable at 1 km² resolution — too coarse for 10 m Sentinel-2 pixels. Would average out the spatial signal the model needs.
PRESSURERMIDROPPEDNear-constant during summer high-pressure episodes (all training scenes are fair-weather). Near-zero variance → near-zero feature importance.
Why only satellite + station features, not full spatial layers? The Leuven.cool dataset gives point-level sensor readings. Spatial raster features (building height, road density, DSM) are useful only if they can be matched to each sensor's exact GPS coordinate — which requires reliable geocoding of ∼600 station IDs. That pre-processing step is left for v3.

⚙️ Data Pipeline — From Raw to Trained Model

End-to-end flow from satellite download to deployed predictor, including where the train/test split happens and how retraining works.

Download
Raw data acquisition

Copernicus OData API → JP2 rasters (B04, B08, B11) per scene date.
Leuven.cool Zenodo CSVs (Q3 per year) → sensor readings with GPS, temperature, humidity.
RMI openkmi API → daily city averages for anomaly baseline.

Feature Extraction
Sentinel-2 → pixel values at sensor coordinates

sample_band_at_coords(jp2, lats, lons) using rasterio + pyproj.
NDVI = (B08−B04)/(B08+B04) · NDBI = (B11−B08)/(B11+B08) · B11 raw.
Scene window: ±7 days around each acquisition date.

Anomaly Computation
temp_anomaly = temp_c − city_daily_mean

City daily mean computed from all Leuven.cool stations for that calendar date.
This removes absolute temperature (driven by weather) and isolates the spatial UHI signal.

Merge & Filter
30 894 890 rows → 5 575 ML rows

Spatial join: match each sensor reading to its closest Sentinel-2 scene within the ±7-day window.
Drop rows where any feature is NaN (cloud mask, missing sensor readings).
Result: one row per (station × scene date) with 8 features + anomaly.

Train / Test Split
80/20 stratified by year (StratifiedShuffleSplit)

Train: 4 460 rows (2023, 2024, 2025 proportionally) · Test: 1 115 rows.
Key fix in v2: StandardScaler fitted on train rows only, then applied to test.
All reported metrics (RMSE, MAE, R²) come from the held-out test set.

Train Models
RF · XGBoost · CNN · Stacked ensemble

5-fold CV for standard RMSE · Leave-One-Station-Out CV for spatial robustness.
Stacked ensemble: OOF predictions (no leakage) → Ridge meta-learner → final model.

Evaluate
Test set · Per-year breakdown · LOSOCV

Final scores reported on the held-out 20%. Per-year table shows 2024 penalty (1 scene).
LOSOCV gap (+0.089°C) quantifies spatial autocorrelation in the sensor network.

Deployment Retrain
Retrain on 100% of data before deployment

Once metrics are accepted, refit the stacked ensemble on all 5 575 rows (no test split).
The test-set evaluation already confirmed generalisation — retraining on full data maximises signal.
Retrained model → anomaly_engine.py → FastAPI / Streamlit dashboard.

⚠️ Netatmo is noisy but usable Netatmo stations are consumer weather stations in private gardens, balconies, and rooftops — not professionally calibrated. The fetch_netatmo.py script already includes outlier filtering (−10°C to +50°C plausibility gate). Key steps before using as training data: (1) compute city_daily_mean from Netatmo itself, (2) filter outlier stations (>3σ from city mean), (3) require ≥3 readings per station per day. Even noisy Netatmo data can add hundreds of ground-truth points per city per scene date.
How to run the Netatmo fetcher Register a free app at dev.netatmo.com, add NETATMO_CLIENT_ID and NETATMO_CLIENT_SECRET to .env, then run:

python scripts/fetch_netatmo.py

This writes data/raw/netatmo/<city>/sensors_YYYY-MM-DD.csv — one row per public station, with latitude, longitude, temperature_c, humidity, timestamp.

Step-by-step to v3 multi-city training:

Step 1 ✓ done
Download T31UES Sentinel-2 scenes

Brussels, Ghent, Antwerp scenes downloaded: 2023-08-10, 2023-08-20, 2024-07-30, 2025-07-01, 2025-08-12.

Step 2
Fetch Netatmo ground truth for all 5 cities

Run python scripts/fetch_netatmo.py on scene dates. Target: ≥50 validated stations per city per scene date.

Step 3
Compute city-specific anomalies

Each city uses its own daily mean baseline (not Leuven's). temp_anomaly = temp_c − this_city_daily_mean. This makes anomalies comparable across cities.

Step 4
Extract satellite features at Netatmo coordinates

Same sample_band_at_coords() pipeline, but pointing at T31UES JP2 files for Brussels/Ghent/Antwerp and T31UFT for Leuven/Liège.

Step 5
Add city_id as a categorical feature

One-hot encode city (or use an ordinal). Lets the model learn that Brussels's baselines differ from Leuven's without conflating spatial patterns.

Step 6
Leave-One-City-Out CV

Train on {Leuven, Brussels, Ghent, Antwerp}, test on Liège. Repeat for each city. This is the gold standard for transfer validation.

⚡ GPU Setup — CNN Is Running on CPU (Fix This Now)

PyTorch 2.11.0+cpu is installed — the CPU-only build. The GPU exists but PyTorch can't see it. 20 epochs on CPU takes ~8 minutes; on GPU it takes ~25 seconds.

Root cause: wrong PyTorch build Running torch.cuda.is_available() returns False and torch.version.cuda is None. The +cpu suffix in the version means CUDA support was stripped at build time. Re-installing with the CUDA build fixes it.

Fix in 3 steps:

Step 1 — Check your GPU and CUDA version. Open a terminal (not VS Code, just Windows Terminal) and run:

nvidia-smi

Look at the top-right corner of the output — it says something like CUDA Version: 12.1. Use that number in step 2.

Step 2 — Uninstall the CPU build, reinstall with CUDA:

# activate the project venv first
.venv\Scripts\activate

# uninstall current CPU build
pip uninstall torch torchvision torchaudio -y

# install CUDA 12.1 build (most common for RTX 20xx/30xx/40xx)
pip install torch torchvision torchaudio --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu121

# if your nvidia-smi says CUDA 11.8 instead, use cu118:
# pip install torch torchvision torchaudio --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu118

Step 3 — Verify in the notebook:

import torch
print(torch.cuda.is_available())   # should print: True
print(torch.cuda.get_device_name(0)) # your GPU name
Once GPU works — bump CNN from 20 to 100 epochs 100 epochs on a Tesla T4 (Google Colab) completed. Final CNN: RMSE 0.5273, MAE 0.3099, R² 0.3691. Loss curve (0.501 → 0.369) was still declining at epoch 20 — the extra 80 epochs confirmed the trend. A dedicated colab_cnn_gpu.ipynb notebook handles upload from Drive and saves weights back as cnn_model_100ep.pt.
XGBoost can also use the GPU In the XGBoost model cell, add one parameter:

xgb.XGBRegressor(..., device='cuda', tree_method='hist')

This speeds up training ~5–10× and enables the RandomizedSearchCV to try more candidates in the same time.

🚀 Future Improvements — Roadmap to v3

Ranked by expected impact on R² and feasibility given the current project stack.

🟢 High impact · Immediately feasible

Multi-city training with Netatmo

Run fetch_netatmo.py on scene dates, merge with T31UES bands, train on all 5 cities. Doubles the training set. Leave-One-City-Out CV validates cross-city transfer. Expected R² gain: +0.03–0.06.

🟢 High impact · Immediately feasible

CNN with GPU — 100 epochs

Done. Trained on Google Colab Tesla T4 via colab_cnn_gpu.ipynb. Final result: RMSE 0.5273, MAE 0.3099, R² 0.3691 (up from 0.3464 at 20 epochs CPU). Weights saved as cnn_model_100ep.pt in Drive.

🟢 High impact · Medium effort

Kriged weather surfaces

Replace station-median HUMIDITY, wind_ms, SOLARRADIATION with spatially interpolated surfaces (kriging or IDW from station coordinates). Grid prediction range widens from ±1.3°C to physically realistic ±4–8°C.

🟡 Medium impact · Medium effort

Landsat 8/9 LST feature

Add Land Surface Temperature from Landsat Band 10 (30 m thermal) as a 9th feature (IMPERVIOUSNESS is now 8th). Requires temporal alignment with Sentinel-2 scenes (same or adjacent day) and reprojection to 10 m. Physically the strongest UHI signal.

🟡 Medium impact · Medium effort

Building height / DSM

NGI Belgium provides a 1-m Digital Surface Model. Derived features: sky-view factor, street canyon ratio, building density per 100 m². These encode the 3D urban morphology that drives nocturnal UHI retention.

🟡 Medium impact · High effort

Temporal LSTM model

Stack multiple scene dates as a time series per station → LSTM predicts anomaly incorporating heat accumulation dynamics. Captures multi-day heat wave build-up that single-scene snapshots miss.

🔵 Lower priority · High effort

Transfer learning for CNN

Pretrain CNN on BigEarthNet (590k Sentinel-2 patches, 43 land-use classes), fine-tune on UHI anomaly. Solves the small-sample problem (5 575 rows) that limits the current CNN vs tree models.

🔵 Lower priority · Medium effort

SHAP explainability layer

Add SHAP values to the deployed API: per-prediction feature attribution showing why a grid cell is hot (high B11? low NDVI? low wind?). Useful for city planners choosing intervention locations.

On R² and what "good" means for UHI regression R² = 0.578 is solid for a small-sample spatial regression on citizen-science data. Published UHI studies using satellite + station fusion typically report R² 0.55–0.75. The structural ceiling here is the weather-feature median approximation and spatial autocorrelation — not the model family. Switching to kriged surfaces alone is likely worth +0.08 R².
